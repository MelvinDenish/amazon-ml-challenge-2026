Counts behind those numbers:

- **17,125 true pairs were retrieved but rejected:** 14,349, or **83.8%**, have empty addresses.

The metric is macro F0.5: recovering an entity's only true match is worth more than adding another member to an already almost-correct set. Rank experiments by actual per-entity score gain, not pair recall or logloss alone.

**Why the empty-address problem needs a different experiment**

Of the 14,349 retrieved empty-address misses, **12,664 (88.3%)** have a true S1 whose normalized core name is shared by another S1 in the full country. **10,663 (74.3%)** share even the normalized full name. Median core-name multiplicity is six in India and four in US. This explains why another name-similarity feature or exact-name flag is unlikely to supply the desired improvement.

This is not proof of irreducibility. Strictly identical target inputs with multiple owner states did not explain these misses in this diagnostic. Different spellings, aliases, and other records may still carry evidence. Conversely, absence of exact duplicate target rows does not prove their owner is identifiable. The useful question is whether another confidently linked record adds discriminating text.

The evidence is specific: an exact folded name in an addressed, predicted-high-confidence member exists for **1,302 missing candidate pairs** and **4,291 retrieved false negatives**. Perfect recovery of the latter subset is worth only about +0.000980 historically. Therefore exact-name propagation alone is both insufficient and already contradicted by the cheap specialist's result. The next model must compare the actual varied records and conflicting owners.

**Workstream 1 — recover addressed records before buying another broad classifier**

Use the TPU runtime's CPU/RAM host for sharded retrieval and feature preparation. This does not require porting the model to TPU/XLA. A historical TPU-host run reported 224 CPUs and built 54.5M base-feature rows in roughly five minutes, but current host resources and throughput must be measured rather than assumed. Do not rerun the old whole-pipeline CPU XGBoost job: its purpose and costs are different.

First, run two fixed hash-sampled query sets per training country against the **full target pool**. Compare each proposal with the exact current candidate IDs. Classify misses into: no surviving key, key suppressed by frequency cap, pool-600 truncation, and final rerank/top-20 loss. The previous pilot merged the first two and excluded reverse-top-3 recovery, so its top-40 gains are not production-incremental estimates.

Implement three small competing proposals:

1. **Learned reranking of the existing pool.** Train a small CPU tree model on representative full-pool examples, including positives outside the current top 20, with name/address similarities, missingness, number compatibility, key scores, and ranks. Keep a union of the normal top set and a small number of strongest address-based or learned-score candidates. The current hand-written reranker simply adds name and address similarity; this can bury a true record whose name is severely corrupted.
2. **Address-first and capped-key backoffs.** Preserve raw compound numbers, street/locality token evidence, and token-order-tolerant address similarity. Handle an over-common single key by combining it with another field, rather than simply lifting all caps. Use an adaptive extra quota for ambiguous or weakly matched queries.
3. **Alias retrieval through addressed anchors.** Use at most two independently high-confidence predicted members as additional query names. Add retrieved pairs to the scoring set; do not declare them matches by transitivity.

Start with an average cap of roughly **5–10 new candidate edges per S1** and bounded shards. This is a compute guard, not a modeling constant. Measure recall, macro candidate-oracle gain, recoveries of otherwise-unmatched S1s, candidate count, and wall time. A useful continuation gate is **at least +0.0015 macro candidate-oracle gain** on both sample replicates with manageable overhead. After real scoring, require at least **+0.0005 macro gain**, with a development milestone of +0.0015 or more. The thresholds are proposed budget decisions, not forecasts.

Preserve the original candidates and base scores initially. Score every new edge explicitly; the original GBM uncertainty band cannot route pairs that never received GBM scores. Train/calibrate the extra-edge scorer using the same new retrieval route and its hard negatives. Do not inject true pairs into held-out retrieval to inflate recall. Training on labeled positives that the old blocker missed is appropriate; forcing them into validation candidates is not.

**Workstream 2 — use S2/S3 text as additional evidence**

Build a bounded **record-to-supporting-record cross-encoder** experiment. The current deployed CE primarily sees S1 versus target. For a difficult target, score it against one or two confidently matched records of each plausible owner. Different records may preserve complementary names or aliases that S1 does not expose clearly.

Training positives come from records sharing a training owner, including S1↔S2/S3 and selected S2↔S3 or within-source positives. Hard negatives come from distinct real owners that compete under the new retrieval, especially same-name/different-address families. Mask same-owner pairs when constructing in-batch negatives. Multiple S2 or S3 matches are allowed; do not invent a one-per-source rule.

Use the existing permitted multilingual encoder family. A pairwise support scorer can retain compact inputs; only lengthen the context if actual truncation measurements justify it. Aggregate the direct CE score, top two support scores, supporting-record confidence, competing-owner scores, and a null-owner option in a small stack. Cap support count and carry contradictory evidence so one wrong anchor does not spread many merges.

Anchor selection must match deployment: predictions produced without the evaluated labels, calibration for high precision, no candidate serving as its own anchor, and no truth-picked supporting member at validation. Separate support construction, model tuning, and final evaluation as far as the existing artifacts allow. The historical nested/record-exposure limitations still apply; report them honestly.

Run a **small pilot first**, using the current CE as an initial scorer of target/support pairs where possible. Compare direct-only versus direct-plus-support on identical owners/candidates. Expand training only if it improves the full macro metric. An initial gate is +0.0005 with improved empty-address errors and no meaningful singleton regression. If the pilot fails, stop this branch rather than spend four hours replicating the earlier exact-name specialist.

**Workstream 3 — learned retrieval only if the first retrieval pilot justifies it**

If address/backoff/reranking recovery cannot expose enough missing truth, train a small multilingual dual encoder using supplied owner labels and real hard negatives. Include S2/S3 positive views so it learns name and alias transformations. Index normalized embeddings by country, union a small nearest-neighbor list with the accepted lexical candidates, and score only the incremental edges.

Supervised contrastive blocking with nearest-neighbor retrieval is established in [SC-Block](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03132); using blocking-derived negatives is supported by [Block-SCL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02008). Those papers motivate the design, not a promised competition gain. Use only the competition data and permitted pretrained models, with no external business lookup.

Benchmark on a training query sample against the full relevant target pool before embedding every record. Stop if semantic retrieval mostly returns the same-name wrong businesses. GPU embedding of the full target universe and test queries must fit inside the allowance; training time alone is not the experiment cost. Use the TPU host for indexing, joins, deduplication, and CPU scoring where appropriate.

**Transfer and final selection**

France is unlabelled, and the current public-to-historical gap is **0.005136**. If that gap happened to remain constant, public 0.988 would correspond to historical 0.993136 and public 0.990 to historical 0.995136. The latter exceeds the present candidate oracle of 0.994524. This arithmetic is only a planning illustration, not a statistical test ceiling, but it shows why improved retrieval and transfer matter together.

Use a compact evaluation matrix: current `ce12x`; plus accepted retrieval; plus support scorer; plus both. Preserve all competitors while selecting and calibrating. Report per-country, singleton, empty-address, near-number/qualifier, and small-match-set performance. Retain a fixed final-check subset for the new work; the repeatedly consulted historical OOF is development evidence, not untouched validation. Use competitor-family groups for paired uncertainty estimates. Any genuine country-transfer experiment must exclude target-country labels from training, not merely slice both-country predictions afterward.

Examine France changes as a risk diagnostic; do not force match counts to resemble US/India or reuse US priors as French truth. Prefer country-agnostic evidence such as address consistency, alias support, and disagreement margins. Do not add a new confidence veto just to make the test distribution look familiar.

The exact-F selector and the corrected other-max feature may be evaluated as cheap finishing ablations, but their measured gains do not justify separate leaderboard probes. Broad CE routing expansion has a small measured oracle return; apply it only to an identified risky slice. A 7B-model or broad hyperparameter sweep has no stronger evidence than the targeted experiments above.
